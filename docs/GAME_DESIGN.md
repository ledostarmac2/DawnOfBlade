# Game Design

## Pillars

1. Exploration: Give players a readable low-poly world with clear paths, landmarks, and points of interest.
2. Skill progression: Reward repeated world actions with XP, levels, unlocks, and new choices.
3. NPC dialogue: Make characters useful, memorable, and connected to quests and learning prompts.
4. Quest-based learning: Teach through tasks, context, and rewards instead of isolated flashcards.
5. Language practice through gameplay: Weave vocabulary prompts into dialogue, items, shops, crafting, and quests.

## Sandbox loop

The playable prototype is an open low-poly overworld reached from a login/account screen: a
customizable humanoid character with click-to-move and an orbit camera, NPCs and dialogue, gathering
and crafting, a 25-skill 99-cap progression, equipment and a shop, combat against hostile actors with
attack styles and a combat triangle, an objective-based quest, and language-learning vocabulary
prompts. Progress is saved per account.

It is built to feel like a classic skill-driven RPG sandbox while heading toward a server-authoritative,
grid-based MMORPG (600 ms tick, chunked open world, classless skills, player-driven economy) — all
original, with no copied maps, names, characters, assets, data, or mechanics tables from proprietary
games. See `docs/ROADMAP.md` for phase history and `docs/PRODUCTION_BACKEND_ARCHITECTURE.md` for the
intended online direction.
